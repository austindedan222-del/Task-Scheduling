Help & User Guide

Everything you need to know about scheduling your tasks.

1 Add your tasks

Press Add Task on the dashboard or the My Tasks page. Give it a title, an optional description, a deadline (date and time of day), a priority from 1 (lowest) to 10 (highest), and a duration with a unit — minutes, hours, days, weeks or months.

2 Generate your schedule

Open Schedule and the greedy algorithm orders your tasks by urgency — high priority and near deadlines come first. You can Export to PDF to keep a copy.

3 Get reminder emails

The dashboard lists what is Due Soon (next 2 days) and Overdue. Click Email My Reminders Now to send yourself a summary email immediately.

4 Watch your limits

If the tasks due on one day add up to more than 24 hours of work, an overload warning appears so you can move something earlier or trim its estimate.

Step-by-step

  • Create an account — click Create Account. An email address is required because reminder emails are sent to it.
  • Add a task — the add/edit form opens as a popup. Title, deadline (date and time), priority (1–10) and duration (value + unit) are required; description is optional.
  • Edit or delete — use the Edit and Delete buttons in the task table on the My Tasks page. You only ever see your own tasks.
  • Generate a schedule — the Schedule page ranks every task by a score of priority and days remaining, then lays them out earliest-first so the most urgent work is done first.
  • Export to PDF — the Schedule page can save the generated order as a PDF file.
  • Log out — the Logout button at the bottom of the sidebar ends your session on this device.

Email reminders

  • Right now: click Email My Reminders Now on the dashboard and a summary of your due-soon and overdue tasks is emailed to your account address straight away.
  • In the background: the send_reminders command checks every account for tasks due within 2 days or already overdue and emails each user once. It is meant to be run on a schedule (cron on Linux/macOS, Task Scheduler on Windows) — see the project README for the exact line to use.
  • Nothing to send? If no task is due soon and none is overdue, no email is sent — you will see a message telling you so.
  • No email address? Accounts without an email address are skipped, so make sure your profile has one.

Workspaces & team supervision

  • Create a workspace — open Workspaces in the sidebar and give it a name. You become its supervisor; supervisors see every member and every task created inside that workspace.
  • Add members — on the workspace page, enter an existing account's username or email, pick a role (Supervisor or Member) and press Add Member. One person can hold a different role in each workspace.
  • Assign a task — supervisors press Assign Task, fill in the form and choose who it goes to. It lands in that member's My Tasks and their own schedule, and appears in Team Overview with its status and progress bar.
  • Team Overview — the supervisor's dashboard of every member's workspace tasks, side by side. It shows status only — it never generates a combined schedule, and each person's schedule is computed from their own tasks alone.
  • Privacy — tasks you add normally are personal: nobody else, not even a supervisor, can see them. Only tasks assigned inside a workspace are overseen by its supervisor.
  • Members can update the status and percent-complete of tasks assigned to them; only supervisors can edit or reassign any workspace task.

Progress tracking

  • Every task has a status — Not Started, In Progress or Completed — shown as a colored badge, plus a progress bar (0–100%) while it is in progress. Set them in the add/edit form.
  • Completed tasks are finished work: they are left out of the generated schedule, the overload warning, and reminder emails.

Tips

  • Be realistic about duration — it drives both the schedule and the overload warning; the system converts any unit (hours, days, weeks, months) to minutes internally.
  • Re-run the schedule after adding or editing tasks so the order stays current.
  • Deadlines include a time of day; a task becomes overdue once its deadline date has passed, and the sooner it is due the higher its urgency.

Still stuck? Add a task and try the dashboard — the system is designed to be explored.
